# The voyage on paper

A test of the voyage map (PLAN M24) before any of it is built. You need a six-sided die
and a pencil. It takes twenty minutes. Play it twice.

**What to look for:** where you had to think, and where you were only rolling. Mark the
dull parts. Rewrite the events in your own voice; mine are drafts.

The numbers are tuned for play, not documented, except where marked.

---

## What you keep track of

| | Start | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Months out** | 0 | The Morgan's first voyage was 40 months. *Documented.* |
| **Water** | 6 | Months of it. Filled only at a port. At 0, a man sickens each month until you reach one |
| **Bread and beef** | 30 | Months of it. At 0 the voyage is over, whatever is in her hold |
| **Hold** | 0 | Barrels of sperm oil. She holds 2,000 |
| **The men** | 32 | Hands aboard. Below 20 you cannot lower more than two boats |
| **Strength** | Fresh | Fresh → Willing → Tiring → Weary → Spent. Spent men cannot lower |
| **Her hull** | Sound | Sound → Strained → Leaking. Leaking, she must go home |
| **Months since fresh food** | 0 | After 8, scurvy (event 2) comes on any roll of 1 |

---

## The route

Move one stop at a time. You may go back the way you came. Each arrow is the months
it costs; take that much water and bread off as you sail.

```
New Bedford ──2──► Fayal (port) ──4──► round the Horn ──1──► Talcahuano (port)
                                                               │
                                     ┌──────────1──────────────┘
                                     ▼
               Offshore Ground (cruising) ──3──► Honolulu (port) ──2──► Japan Ground (cruising)
                                     │                                        │
                                     └──────── home, 6 months ◄───────────────┘
```

Rounding the Horn: roll once. On a 1 or 2, read event 8.

---

## On a cruising ground

Stay as many months as you like. Each month, take off water and bread, step Strength
down by one, and roll:

| Roll | |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing raised. The mastheads see only sea |
| 2, 3 | **There she blows.** Lower, or let her go by (if Spent, you must let her go) |
| 4 | An event: roll again and read that number below |
| 5 | A gale. Hull steps down one, unless you heave to and lose the month's roll next month |
| 6 | A sail on the horizon: read event 1, the gam |

**Lowering.** Roll for the chase:

| Roll | |
|---|---|
| 1 | A boat stove. Roll again: on a 1, a man is lost |
| 2 | The iron draws. Nothing |
| 3 | She sounds, and the line runs out. Nothing, and a line lost |
| 4 | A Nantucket sleigh ride: she tows the boat to windward all afternoon, and dies. Roll a die, ×10, +20 barrels. Strength down one more |
| 5, 6 | Fast, lanced, and dead. Roll a die, ×10, +20 barrels |

Cutting-in and trying-out a whale costs one more step of Strength.

## At a port

Fill the water to 6. Months since fresh food back to 0. Strength back to Fresh. Then roll
for the men who run: **Fayal**, lose 1 on a 1. **Talcahuano**, lose one man on each of
two dice showing 1–3. **Honolulu**, lose a man for every point over 2 on one die. On a 1 on a fresh die, read event 7 before you sail. You may
ship green hands to make up the number, one for one.

---

## The events

*Drafts, meant to be rewritten.*

**1. The gam.** A New Bedford ship, eighteen months out, heaves to and her master comes
aboard with the letters. He says the Japan Ground was greasy in the spring.
- *Spend the day gamming.* Lose nothing. You learn where the whales are: your next roll
  on the Japan Ground counts 2 as 3 and 1 as 2.
- *Keep cruising.* One more roll this month.

**2. Scurvy.** Two men in the forecastle with soft gums and old wounds opening.
- *Make for the nearest port*, straight away, wherever it is.
- *Carry on.* Two men off duty; on the next 1 you roll, one of them dies.

**3. The green hand.** A boy shipped at Fayal asks to go in the boat instead of keeping
ship. He has been aloft every trick and never once been slow.
- *Put him in the boat.* On your next lowering, a 1 is a 2 — he pulls like a man.
  But if it comes up 1 anyway, he is the one lost.
- *Keep him aboard.* Nothing, and he stops asking.

**4. The forecastle.** Two men fought over a stolen plug of tobacco and one has a knife
cut. The mate wants one of them flogged at the gangway.
- *Flog him.* The next port, add one to the men who run.
- *Stop his grog and let it lie.* The next time the men are Weary, one refuses duty:
  Strength down one more.

**5. Man overboard.** Reefing in a squall at night; a hand is off the fore yard.
- *Round to and lower a boat.* Roll: 3 or better, you have him back. Lose the month's roll.
- *Keep on.* He is lost. Nothing else is.

**6. The carpenter's report.** Two boats are stove past mending; one spare on the skids.
- *Put in to the next port to build a new one.* It costs an extra month there.
- *Cruise with three boats.* A 1 on any lowering now also loses the boat for good.

**7. The deserters.** Only in port. Four men mean to take a whaleboat in the night and
run inland. The boatsteerer tells you.
- *Double the anchor watch.* No one runs this time. Strength down one.
- *Let them go and ship others.* Lose four, ship four green hands.

**8. Off the Horn.** Three weeks of westerly gales, and she has not made fifty miles.
- *Keep beating.* Hull steps down one. Strength down two.
- *Heave to and wait.* One more month, the water with it.

## Coming home: the account

For every barrel, the oil fetches **$29.61** (31½ gallons at 94 cents, 1841), and
**$27.83** after the charges of the voyage.

| | Lay | Take away for slops |
|---|---|---|
| You, the master | 1/15 *(documented)* | — |
| The first mate | 1/20 | — |
| An able seaman | 1/150 | $2 a month |
| A green hand | 1/190 | $2 a month |
| The cabin boy | 1/300 *(documented)* | $2 a month |

Work out what the boy comes home with. Then:

- **1,500 barrels or more:** a greasy voyage. The owners give you the ship again.
- **500 to 1,500:** she paid her way. They will think about it.
- **Under 500:** a broken voyage. You will not have a ship of theirs again.

For measure: the Morgan came home from her first voyage, 1845, with 1,600 barrels of
sperm oil after forty months. *Documented.*
